About

I work at the intersection of wireless communications, statistical signal processing, machine learning, and security. My research develops theory-guided methods for resilient communication, distributed sensing and fusion, MIMO and high-frequency systems, coding, information theory, and integrated sensing and communications.

I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Purdue University, my M.S. from KAIST, and my B.S. from Sogang University. Before joining Arizona State University in 2024, I was on the faculty at the University of Kansas and City University of Hong Kong and worked as a researcher at Nokia Bell Labs, KTH, and ETRI.

30 issued U.S. patents $8M external funding as PI IEEE Senior Member

Research

Research Areas

Resilient and Secure Communications

Physical-layer security, anti-jamming methods, secret sharing, robust coding, and zero-trust approaches for 5G and beyond.

Distributed Sensing and Fusion

Distributed learning, over-the-air aggregation, compressed transmission, spectrum intelligence, and resource-aware sensor fusion.

Machine Learning for Communications

Theory-guided learning for coding, detection, channel estimation, federated learning, and adaptive wireless systems.

MIMO, Millimeter-Wave, and THz Systems

Massive MIMO, cell-free architectures, hybrid precoding, beam alignment, channel estimation, and high-frequency network design.

Information and Coding Theory

Feedback and two-way communication, quantum LDPC decoding, approximate message passing, and short-blocklength coding.

Integrated Sensing and Communications

Radar waveform and beam design, sensing parameter estimation, and spectrum-compatible joint communication and sensing.
